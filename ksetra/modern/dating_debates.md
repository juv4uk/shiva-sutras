# Dating the Mahābhāṣya

This document tracks the academic debates regarding the dating of Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya*, which is Witness 1 (W1) in our epistemic framework. 

## Traditional Dating

The traditional view often places Patañjali around **~150 BCE**. This is based on internal evidence, such as references to the Mauryan empire and the Pushyamitra Shunga's sacrifices (e.g., *iha puṣpamitraṃ yājayāmaḥ*).

## Modern Academic Scholarship

Contemporary scholarship has raised questions about this early date, suggesting a later period, often around **100–150 CE** (or even later in some extreme theories). 

Key voices in the debate:
- **Johannes Bronkhorst**: Argues that the text we have today might have been redacted later, and points out the complexities in assuming a single, unadulterated composition date in the 2nd century BCE.
- **Harry Falk**: Discusses epigraphic and historical evidence that challenges the straightforward identification of Patañjali's contemporary references.
- **Michael Witzel**: Analyzes the geographic and linguistic evidence, often supporting a nuanced view of the text's formation over time.

## Implications for the Śiva-sūtras

While the exact date of Patañjali does not change the internal logic or the *apauruṣeya* (authorless/structural) nature of the Śiva-sūtras canon itself, it is critical for tracking the chronological depth of our witnesses (W1). 

In our `source-ladder.yaml`, the Mahābhāṣya remains our earliest solid anchor for the *exact sequence* of the sutras, regardless of whether it crystallized in 150 BCE or 150 CE. We explicitly track this uncertainty as part of our epistemic contract.
